Strength is solved. Hands aren’t.

Robots have lifted heavy things for decades. Picking up an egg without cracking it is still the hard part.

Hasta service robot

Hasta is a service robot designed from the fingertips back. It sees what it is about to touch, closes on it with less force than you would use, and passes it to a person the way a person would.

Twenty motors in each hand. One job: get it there whole.

Hands

Every finger moves on its own.

Twenty motors in each hand, and a touch sensor under every pad.

Vision

First, it looks.

Before a finger moves, it knows what the egg is, how far away it sits and where each fingertip will go.

Grip

Firm enough to lift. Gentle enough not to crack.

An egg cracks at about 45 newtons. Hasta holds it with less than one in each fingertip.

Touch

Its fingertips can feel.

Ribbed pads read pressure 1,000 times a second and catch a slip before it shows.

Made for people

It hands things over the way a person would.

Slowly, palm up, at a height you can reach.

Hasta in hospitals and hotels

Built for the rounds nobody has time for.

Meals, medicines and linen, carried down the corridor, so your staff can stay with people.

Where it works

Wherever
hands are short.

One robot. The same careful grip in every building.

Hasta does the carrying that pulls trained people away from the people they trained for. It fits through a standard door, takes the lift, and works the same corridor twice without getting bored.

Hospital wards

Medicine cups and water to the bedside, for the right patient, so nurses stay on the ward.

Care homes

A cup of tea held out slowly, palm up, at chair height. The fortieth round as gentle as the first.

Hotels

A covered plate carried level from the kitchen to the door, at any hour.

Laboratories

Sample jars moved between benches without a tremor or a dropped rack.

Kitchens

Lifted from the cup and set down whole. Eggs survive the trip.
